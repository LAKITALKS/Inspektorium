## Case material (claim + available evidence)

Researchers at the University of Pennsylvania randomly assigned 143 college students either to continue their usual use of Facebook, Snapchat and Instagram or to limit each platform to ten minutes per day. Over three weeks they collected objective usage data from phone screenshots and measured depression and loneliness. Students who restricted social-media use reported significant declines in depressive symptoms and loneliness compared with the control group. The authors caution that the study involved only college students and three specific platforms and that the results may not generalise to other populations.
