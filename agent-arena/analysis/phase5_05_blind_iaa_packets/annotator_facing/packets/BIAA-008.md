## Case material (claim + available evidence)

Researchers studying Alzheimer's disease in genetically engineered mice discovered that severe drops in a molecule called NAD+ disrupt the brain's energy balance. They treated mice that already had advanced Alzheimer's by restoring NAD+ levels to a healthy range using a compound. The treatment repaired brain damage and the animals regained normal memory performance. The scientists said these results suggest that boosting NAD+ could move Alzheimer's therapy beyond slowing decline toward meaningful recovery and called for clinical trials to see if the approach will translate to patients.
