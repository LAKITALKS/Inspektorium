## Case material (claim + available evidence)

A six-month experiment across 141 companies in six countries tested whether cutting the work week from five days to four while maintaining pay could improve employee well-being. Companies volunteered to restructure their workflows so that productivity would not decline with fewer hours. Nearly 2,900 participants completed surveys before and after the trial. After six months, workers reported lower stress and burnout, higher job satisfaction, and no perceived drop in performance. More than 90 percent of participating companies chose to continue the four-day schedule. The researchers cautioned that because companies self-selected into the trial and outcomes were self-reported, further randomised evaluations are needed.
