## Rationale
- **Why this is the authoritative evaluated claim:** it is the study's own lead author
  converting an observational association into an actionable causal benefit ("can provide
  substantial health benefits", "can make a real difference"). That is the exact claim/evidence
  mismatch the case is designed to test.
- **Why the study finding is evidence context (not the claim):** the paper reports an
  *association* and explicitly states "Observational design — cannot prove causation"; read on
  its own it is honestly scoped (`solid`). It is the evidence against which the claim is judged.
- **Why other headlines/quotations are context (not additional claims):** the three media
  headlines are interchangeable publisher paraphrases of the same finding (secondary voice); the
  co-author quotation uses cautious "associated with" language and would not carry the causal
  overreach. They are retained as additional context, explicitly marked as not the evaluated
  claim, so no material is removed.
- **Why the existing gold label still applies:** the isolated claim frames an observational
  association as an actionable causal benefit → `correlation_vs_causation` (unchanged).
- **Strongest plausible alternative reading:** `overclaim` — "can provide substantial health
  benefits" / "can make a real difference" is also rhetorical inflation of a modest,
  hedged-in-the-paper association.
- **Why the alternative remains secondary:** the core structural error is drawing a *causal /
  actionable* conclusion from a non-causal observational design (the specific
  `correlation_vs_causation` trigger); the rhetorical inflation is the accompanying generic
  layer, recorded as the secondary reading, consistent with the unchanged gold.
