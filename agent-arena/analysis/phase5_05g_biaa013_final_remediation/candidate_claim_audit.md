# Phase 5.05g — Candidate-Claim Audit (BIAA-013)

Read only committed source material (BIAA-013_v2, verbatim with its neutral placeholders).
Six distinct candidate statements were identified. No wording is combined across candidates.

Gold label (internal, for curator context only): `correlation_vs_causation` — the forensic
issue is causal/actionable framing of an observational association.

---

### C1 — Study finding / conclusion (the paper itself)
- **Verbatim:** "Participants with >4% of physical activity classified as vigorous had 29-63% lower risk of 8 major chronic diseases…" plus the stated limitations, incl. "Observational design — cannot prove causation".
- **Speaker/voice:** the authors' peer-reviewed paper.
- **Unambiguous attribution:** yes.
- **Complete standalone public claim:** it is a reported *association* with explicit causal caveats — not an overreaching claim.
- **Evidence directly evaluates it:** yes (it *is* the study).
- **Preserves historical evaluated object:** no — the historical issue is the *causal framing*, not the honest association.
- **Preserves gold rationale:** no — read alone, the hedged finding is `solid`, not `correlation_vs_causation`.
- **Risks:** selecting it would change the evaluated object and contradict the gold. → **evidence context, not the claim.**

### C2 — Headline 1 ([News Outlet A] / [Professional Society A] press release)
- **Verbatim:** "Just a few minutes of effort could lower your risk of 8 major diseases"
- **Speaker/voice:** a news outlet / press release (publisher).
- **Unambiguous attribution:** publisher-level, but one of three near-identical headlines.
- **Complete standalone claim:** yes (causal-ish, hedged with "could").
- **Evidence directly evaluates it:** yes (observational cohort).
- **Preserves historical object / gold:** partially — media causal framing, but "could" is hedged and it is one of three interchangeable headlines.
- **Risks:** arbitrary choice among three headlines; publisher voice weaker/less canonical than the researcher's own causal claim.

### C3 — Headline 2 ([News Outlet B])
- **Verbatim:** "Just a Few Breathless Minutes a Day Could Slash Your Risk of 8 Major Diseases"
- Same profile as C2 (publisher voice, "Could", one of three). Same arbitrariness risk.

### C4 — Headline 3 ([News Outlet C])
- **Verbatim:** "Just A Few Minutes of Hard Exercise May Slash Your Risk for These Serious Health Conditions, Study Says"
- Same profile as C2/C3 (publisher voice, "May"). Same arbitrariness risk.

### C5 — Lead author [Author B] quotation  ← strongest single evaluated claim
- **Verbatim:** "Our findings suggest that making some of your physical activity vigorous can provide substantial health benefits. This doesn't require going to the gym. Adding short bursts of activity that make you slightly breathless into daily life can make a real difference."
- **Speaker/voice:** the study's **lead author** ([Author B]) — a single, unambiguous speaker.
- **Unambiguous attribution:** yes (one named role, one continuous quotation).
- **Complete standalone claim:** yes — an actionable causal benefit claim.
- **Evidence directly evaluates it:** yes — the observational cohort is exactly the evidence for/against it.
- **Preserves historical evaluated object:** yes — the promoted **causal/actionable** claim drawn from observational data is the historical forensic issue.
- **Preserves gold rationale:** yes — "can provide substantial health benefits / can make a real difference" asserts causal benefit from an observational association → `correlation_vs_causation`.
- **Risks:** contains a decorative hedge ("Our findings suggest") that does not constrain the actionable causal implication (documented as the secondary reading).

### C6 — Co-author [Author D] quotation
- **Verbatim:** "Four and a half minutes are associated with approximately 35 to 50% lower risk for incident cardiovascular disease."
- **Speaker/voice:** a co-author ([Author D]).
- **Unambiguous attribution:** yes.
- **Complete standalone claim:** yes.
- **Preserves historical object / gold:** **no** — uses appropriately cautious correlation language ("associated with"); read alone it does **not** frame causally, so it would not preserve the `correlation_vs_causation` gold. → context, not the evaluated claim.

---

## Audit conclusion
Only **C5 (lead author [Author B] quotation)** is a verbatim, single-speaker, complete claim
that the existing evidence directly evaluates **and** whose selection preserves both the
historical evaluated object and the current gold label. C1 and C6 are honest/hedged (would
imply `solid`); C2–C4 are interchangeable publisher headlines (arbitrary, weaker voice). C5
is carried to STEP 5 for condition-by-condition selection.
