## Why this claim is preferred over the alternatives
- **The study's cautious finding:** it is **evidence context, not the evaluated claim** — the
  paper reports an *association* and explicitly states "Observational design — cannot prove
  causation"; read alone it is honestly scoped and would not carry the forensic issue.
- **Media headlines:** they represent **separate publisher voices** (three interchangeable
  paraphrases) and are **not the authoritative evaluated claim** for v3.
- **The Co-Author quotation:** it is **more cautious** ("associated with …") and would **alter
  the forensic target** away from the causal-framing issue.
- **The selected Lead-Author quotation preserves the historical
  correlation-versus-causation issue:** it converts an observational association into an
  actionable causal benefit ("can provide substantial health benefits", "can make a real
  difference"), which is exactly the claim/evidence mismatch the case is designed to test.
