## 1. Frozen scope and decision rule

Only the following surviving negative-search propositions were rechecked:

- P-02 — arthritis;
- P-05 — headache / migraine;
- V-01 — varicose veins / venous insufficiency;
- E-02 — brain fog / cognitive fog;
- I-02 — immune function.

Menopause and the general sleep, pain, cardiovascular, inflammation and recovery literatures were not reopened.

The intervention vocabulary was expanded from `grounding` / `earthing` to `barefoot`, `bare feet`, `barefoot walking`, `barefoot exercise`, `soil contact`, `direct Earth contact`, `direct contact with (the) Earth`, `direct ground contact`, `Earth-surface contact`, `grounded walking` and `conductive Earth contact`. Condition terms were adapted to each proposition, including relevant synonyms and required endpoints.

A candidate qualified only if it involved conductive contact with Earth/soil or explicitly theorised/tested the intervention as Earth grounding/earthing **and** measured the proposition-specific human condition or endpoint. Ordinary barefoot exercise, forest walking, nature therapy, hiking, exercise rehabilitation, foot reflexology, footwear/biomechanics studies and sensory barefoot training were excluded unless that Earth-contact criterion was independently met. Biomarker observations were not promoted to validated immune function.

## 4. Candidate-level disposition

### P-02 — arthritis

The strongest terminology-generated records were condition-relevant but intervention-ineligible:

- **Shakoor & Block 2006**, “Walking barefoot decreases loading on the lower extremity joints in knee osteoarthritis” (PMID 16947448), compared gait mechanics while walking barefoot versus in shoes. It did not establish contact with a conductive natural surface and did not theorise/test earthing.
- **Neogi et al. 2021**, “Is being barefoot, wearing shoes and physical activity associated with knee osteoarthritis pain flares?” (PMID 33200581), was an observational footwear-exposure analysis, not an Earth-contact intervention.
- “Masai Barefoot Technology” records concerned unstable footwear, not bare-skin Earth contact.

No arthritis-specific human Earth-contact/earthing trial measuring disease activity, symptoms, function or progression was located.

**Classification: A — NEGATIVE SEARCH STATE CONFIRMED**

**NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED.** Searches included structured grounding/earthing terminology and a bounded expanded terminology check for alternative Earth-contact/barefoot intervention descriptions. Absence remains a bounded search finding, not proof that no relevant study exists.

### P-05 — headache / migraine

Expanded searches mostly retrieved infections associated with barefoot soil exposure, ordinary migraine exercise studies, foot reflexology and records where “grounding” had a non-earthing meaning. A 2024 migraine pilot comparing indoor exercise with exercise in a natural environment did not require barefoot Earth contact and reported no condition-by-time effects on HIT-6, MIDAS, migraine days or pain intensity. Reflexology trials mechanically stimulated the feet but did not test grounding.

No headache- or migraine-specific Earth-contact/earthing intervention with frequency, intensity, duration, disability or medication outcomes was located.

**Classification: A — NEGATIVE SEARCH STATE CONFIRMED**

**NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED.** Searches included structured grounding/earthing terminology and a bounded expanded terminology check for alternative Earth-contact/barefoot intervention descriptions. Absence remains a bounded search finding, not proof that no relevant study exists.

### V-01 — varicose veins / venous insufficiency

The only PubMed title/abstract result, **Tsimba et al. 2023** (PMID 37473870), compared barefoot, shod and insole treadmill walking in 52 healthy volunteers and measured plantar venous-pump ejection by plethysmography. Barefoot treadmill walking is ordinary biomechanical testing: there was no conductive Earth exposure, no earthing theory, and no varicose-vein or chronic-venous-insufficiency population. Other hits concerned exercise, compression, neuromuscular stimulation or venous assessment.

No human Earth-contact/earthing study measuring varicose-vein symptoms, duplex anatomy, venous reflux or disease progression was located.

**Classification: A — NEGATIVE SEARCH STATE CONFIRMED**

**NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED.** Searches included structured grounding/earthing terminology and a bounded expanded terminology check for alternative Earth-contact/barefoot intervention descriptions. Absence remains a bounded search finding, not proof that no relevant study exists.

### E-02 — brain fog / cognitive fog

The terminology expansion did locate **Kim et al. 2024**, “Barefoot walking improves cognitive ability in adolescents” (PMID 38926837; PMCID PMC11211751). Fifty-nine 13-year-old boys were analysed after allocation to control, sneaker-walking or barefoot-walking groups. The exercise groups walked for 40 minutes four times weekly for 12 weeks; the programme took place on a playground. The paper explains the intervention through exercise, toe/plantar stimulation and multisensory input. It does not report or test ground conductivity, an electrical connection to Earth, earthing exposure or electron transfer. It is therefore ordinary barefoot exercise under the frozen screening rule. Separately, it measured EEG-derived variables, spatial perception and memory-task variables—not a defined brain-fog or cognitive-fog construct.

The Barefoot-OCD item was a hypothesis paper about a different condition; qualitative barefoot/earthing records supplied no clinical brain-fog endpoint.

**Classification: A — NEGATIVE SEARCH STATE CONFIRMED**

**NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED.** Searches included structured grounding/earthing terminology and a bounded expanded terminology check for alternative Earth-contact/barefoot intervention descriptions. Absence remains a bounded search finding, not proof that no relevant study exists.

### I-02 — immune function

Two human barefoot-walking biomarker papers required closer inspection:

1. **Kim et al. 2024**, “Effects of Barefoot Walking in Urban Forests on CRP, IFNγ, and Serotonin Levels” (PMID 39684992; PMCID PMC11640920), randomised 62 healthy adults after stratification to sneaker and barefoot groups. Both groups walked the same 4.4 km forest route for 90 minutes, four times weekly for five weeks. The route comprised sandy loam and red clay and was created as an “earthing road”; the paper explicitly discusses an earthing/conduction rationale. This satisfies the Earth-contact intervention screen. It does **not** satisfy I-02's endpoint screen. IFN-γ was a salivary biomarker described as an NK-cell indicator, not a validated measure of immune competence. The barefoot group's pre-to-final IFN-γ comparison was not significant (p=.167); the shod group was also not significant pre-to-final (p=.082), and the statistically significant pre-to-session-10 change occurred in the shod group (p=.004), not the barefoot group (p=.115). Infection incidence, vaccine response, pathogen clearance and functional immune outcomes were not measured.
2. **Ho et al. 2021**, “Effects of Barefoot Walking Exercise on Female Hormone, Brain Nerve Growth Factors and Immunoglobulins in Postmenopausal Women” (DOI 10.31216/BDL.20210040), reports 11 participants per barefoot/shod walking group over 12 weeks. The accessible primary record reports concentrations of IgG, IgA and IgM. IgA and IgM were not significantly affected by group or duration; the duration effect for IgG was reported for the shoe-walking group, while IgM changed over time in the barefoot group. These are circulating marker concentrations, not validated immune function. The accessible abstract did not establish the walking surface or verify conductive Earth contact. The KCI/KOAJ primary abstract and reference list were accessible, but a stable article-body/PDF route was not exposed: **TOOL UNAVAILABLE — SKIPPED**. This limitation does not change the disposition because the primary record is sufficient to establish the endpoint mismatch.

Urban indoor gardening and short-term soil-contact microbiome studies were excluded because they tested microbial/biodiversity exposure rather than electrical Earth grounding. They also did not test the advertisement's immune-function proposition.

**Classification: A — NEGATIVE SEARCH STATE CONFIRMED**

**NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED.** Searches included structured grounding/earthing terminology and a bounded expanded terminology check for alternative Earth-contact/barefoot intervention descriptions. Absence remains a bounded search finding, not proof that no relevant study exists.

## 5. H33 terminology lesson

H33 demonstrates that terminology heterogeneity can reduce recall in title/abstract-based searches. For interventions without standardized nomenclature, INSPEKTORIUM should combine controlled vocabulary searches with broader semantic/full-text discovery before declaring search saturation.

This is a methodological finding. It does not establish that PubMed or structured databases are unreliable. The appropriate correction is a layered discovery strategy plus strict intervention verification, not abandonment of structured routes.
