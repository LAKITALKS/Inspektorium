### 20. Terminology / Indexing Drift finding

**TERMINOLOGY / INDEXING DRIFT** means that a relevant intervention may be indexed or described using language different from the obvious target-intervention vocabulary, reducing recall in title/abstract searches. H33 was framed primarily as “barefoot walking,” was missed by the original grounding/earthing title/abstract route, and caused a genuine E-03 state correction when found by a broader route.

**H33 demonstrates that terminology heterogeneity can reduce recall in title/abstract-based searches. For interventions without standardized nomenclature, INSPEKTORIUM should combine controlled vocabulary searches with broader semantic/full-text discovery before declaring search saturation.** This methodological finding does not establish that PubMed is unreliable, that structured databases are inadequate, or that broad web search is superior.

### 21. Search-scope limitations

The expanded terminology robustness check was conducted only for the five propositions retaining a NO MATERIAL DIRECT EVIDENCE LOCATED state after Targeted Evidence Closure. It does not establish equivalent terminology robustness for every proposition in this case.

The remaining propositions were not reopened. Ordinary barefoot exercise, forest walking, nature therapy, hiking, rehabilitation, foot reflexology and sensory training were excluded unless conductive Earth contact or explicit earthing/grounding theory was independently established.

All admitted scientific sources existed on or before 22 August 2026. Closure/robustness execution on 23 August did not admit later evidence. Current product pages were retained only as noncontemporaneous product-family context and were not backdated to the complained-about 2025 advertisement.

The appropriate status is **BOUNDED SEARCH SATURATION WITHIN DOCUMENTED ROUTES AND TERMINOLOGY**. It does not mean that no undiscovered record exists or that the literature is permanently complete. ### 64. Original negative-search protocol

Artifact B used a human direct-evidence criterion: conductive Earth grounding/earthing or direct barefoot Earth contact plus a condition-relevant clinical or functional endpoint. It combined `grounding` and `earthing` with six condition families, applied the 22 August 2026 cut-off, and excluded reviews, testimonials, psychological grounding, electrical-safety uses, animal work, unrelated electrical therapies and generic biomarkers as direct condition evidence.

### 66. Search routes

- PubMed title/abstract with date ceiling;
- Europe PMC title/abstract;
- OpenAlex phrase/condition discovery;
- bounded broad scholarly/web screening;
- in the later robustness check, Europe PMC body/full-text pairing and expanded semantic terminology.

Optional AI research tools were not required. No fourth broad pass was performed.

### 67. Screening depth

Original focused searches requested/screened up to 20 ranked records per structured query; general scholarly/web screening used the first 10 relevant results per condition because total counts were not reliably exposed. The robustness check screened up to 25 PubMed/Europe PMC results per route and up to 20 OpenAlex results per query, stopping when variants yielded duplicates or ineligible barefoot/exercise/nature/soil-exposure noise. Counts are route-level, not unique-study totals.

### 68. H33 exception

H33 invalidated the original menopause negative state. Its title foregrounded “barefoot walking”; the article body established barefoot soil-contact grounding relevance. The study is eligible by date and condition but highly confounded. E-03 was repaired to **VERY LIMITED DIRECT EVIDENCE — GROUNDING COMPONENT NOT ISOLABLE**. Menopause is not retained in the final no-direct-evidence list.

### 69. Expanded terminology protocol

The robustness check added: barefoot, bare feet, barefoot walking, barefoot exercise, soil contact, direct Earth contact, direct contact with the Earth, direct ground contact, Earth-surface contact, grounded walking and conductive Earth contact.
